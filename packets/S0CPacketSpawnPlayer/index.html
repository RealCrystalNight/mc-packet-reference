Notes

This is received when another player enters the client's render distance. ESP modules add the entity to their target list. AntiBot modules check the UUID against tab list entries.

Deep Dive

Role in the protocol

S0C spawns a player entity: VarInt entity id, UUID, fixed-point x/y/z as ints scaled by 32, byte yaw and pitch scaled by 256/360, a short current-item id, then the DataWatcher metadata. EntityTrackerEntry constructs it when a player enters render distance. The client builds an EntityOtherPlayerMP from it. MessageSerializer carries a no-op special case for S0C in the bundled 1.8 sources, a decompile artifact of the 1.7.x-era encoding special case.

Vanilla handling

NetHandlerPlayClient.handleSpawnPlayer decodes the fixed-point coordinates, reads the metadata and registers the entity in the world. From then on the entity is driven by S14 relative moves and S18 teleports. ESP and nametag modules do not hook S0C in these clients: they iterate the world entity list, which is why S0C only shows up in anti-bot and meme modules.

Why clients touch it

S0C is the only packet that announces a new player with a position, which makes it the anti-bot veto point. Spicy's Antibot cancels the packet outright when the spawn looks like a bot (within 17 blocks, more than 1 block above the player, not at the player's coordinates) on non-Hypixel servers; the Hypixel branch is commented out because Hypixel bots carry plausible spawn data, so bots are removed from the entity list per-tick instead. Sigma 4.11 AntiBot inspects spawn packets the same way. The Spicy meme modules (DougDimmadome, FloofyFoxes, Furries) import S0C for skin and model replacement but ship empty handlers.

Exploit surface

Cancelling S0C is client-only: the server keeps simulating the bot, but the local world never receives the entity, so aim and ESP never lock onto it. The risk is entity-id desync if later packets reference the cancelled id, which vanilla guards by simply not spawning the entity. Anti-cheats do not measure S0C; anti-bot heuristics are purely client-side (spawn position relative to the player, invisibility plus name quirks, tab-list absence), and servers that want bots to survive these filters just spawn them with realistic positions, which is exactly what Hypixel does.

Server-Side Handling

EntityTrackerEntry.getPacket constructs S0C from the tracked EntityPlayer with x, y and z scaled by 32, yaw and pitch scaled by 256/360, the held item id as a short, and the DataWatcher data. The server does no per-recipient filtering beyond the tracker's render-distance logic.

Protocol Analysis

Fields: VarInt entity id, UUID (most and least significant longs), three ints scaled by 32, two bytes scaled by 256/360, a short item id (0 when empty), then DataWatcher metadata. The scaling is why the client divides by 32 and multiplies by 360/256. S0C is the only spawn packet with a UUID, which is what makes it the natural hook for anti-bot and name-based filtering.

Anti-Cheat Landscape

No anti-cheat reads S0C. The client-side heuristics in Spicy (17-block range, height above the player, non-coincident coordinates) and the Hypixel per-tick removal (invisibility, name formatting, tab-list absence, Watchdog naming) are the whole anti-bot landscape in this corpus, and servers defeat them by making spawn data indistinguishable from real players.

implementation Implementation Cases

The player-spawn announcement: fixed-point position, byte rotations, item id and metadata. AntiBot (Sigma 4.11, Spicy)

Veto bots at the spawn point: cancel S0C for suspicious spawns so the entity never enters the local world and cannot be targeted.

Spicy hooks EventPacket pre: on non-Hypixel servers, a spawn within 17 blocks, more than 1 block above the player and not at the player's exact coordinates is cancelled with a chat notice; on Hypixel the packet path is commented out and hypixelAntibot() removes bots from the entity list per-tick using invisibility, name formatting, tab-list absence and Watchdog naming heuristics. Sigma inspects incoming S0C the same way on its own packet bus.
