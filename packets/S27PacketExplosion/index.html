Notes

Affected blocks are sent as byte triplets (x, y, z) relative to the explosion center. The client applies particle effects and knockback to the local player.

Deep Dive

Role in the protocol

S27 is the server's explosion broadcast: center x/y/z floats, strength, a list of affected block positions encoded as byte triplets relative to the center, and a per-player knockback vector as three floats. WorldServer sends one S27 per affected player (corpus line 901), with the knockback floats computed per player from the explosion's player knockback map. The client both renders the destroyed blocks and applies the knockback vector to the local player, which is why it is the second half of every velocity module's packet filter alongside S12PacketEntityVelocity.

Vanilla handling

NetHandlerPlayClient.handleExplosion builds an Explosion object from the center and strength, calls doExplosionB(true) to spawn particles and sounds, then adds the packet's motion floats straight into thePlayer.motionX/Y/Z. Critically, the knockback is applied unconditionally on the main thread; there is no entity-ID check because the packet is per-player. That makes S27 the standard fallback for anti-cheat velocity implementations: servers that hide S12 knockback in explosion packets still deliver S27, so clients must filter both.

Why clients touch it

S27 is the most-touched clientbound packet in this batch. Every velocity module in the corpus filters it: Memeware and Nekoware cancel it in a shared combat/Velocity pattern, Rise 5.99's Velocity scales or cancels it, Sigma's AntiVelocity handles it in basic mode, Spicy's AntiKnockback cancels it, and Tenacity's VelocityModule scales it by the same horizontal/vertical settings as S12. Rise 6.x splits this into a suite of per-server modes (Standard, Vulcan, Redesky, UniversoCraft, BufferAbuse), all of which touch S27. Movement modules also consume it: Rise 5.99's Fly (Minemenclub) and LongJump (Hycraft, Vulcan) cancel S27 to control knockback timing, and Sigma's Fly uses it for explosion-based boosting.

Exploit surface

The exploit surface is bidirectional. Client-side, scaling or cancelling the knockback floats is invisible to the server because S27 is one-way; anti-cheats can only infer from resulting motion, which is why velocity modules keep the S12 and S27 paths symmetric. Server-side, a hostile server can send S27 with huge motion floats (Rise's ExplosionCheck guards against motion >= Byte.MAX_VALUE) or enormous affected-block lists to stall the client's block-position loop, which is exactly the flood Tenacity's AntiExploit rate-limits. Rise's ItemDamageComponent also counts S27 as a damage source to resync the player's health model.

Server-Side Handling

WorldServer.createExplosion computes the affected block list and a per-player knockback Vec3 from the blast, then sends a dedicated S27 to each EntityPlayerMP (corpus line 901). The knockback map is filled only for players the explosion actually hits, so players outside the blast receive an S27 with zero motion floats but still get the visual. No client acknowledgement and no rate limiting in vanilla.

Protocol Analysis

Wire format: three floats (center), float strength, int record count, then count byte triplets as offsets from the center (each block position is center + signed byte), then three floats of player motion. The triplet encoding caps the block list extent at 127 blocks from the center, which is why ExplosionCheck thresholds at Byte.MAX_VALUE. PLAY state only.

Anti-Cheat Landscape

Anti-cheats cannot see the mutation, so they infer velocity removal from motion deltas: after a flagged hit they compare the player's acceleration against the expected knockback curve. That is why legit-looking modes exist: Tenacity's horizontal/vertical percentages, Rise 6.x's per-server modes, and movement modules that cancel S27 only while airborne. The Rise 6.x BufferAbuse and PingSpoofComponent keep the S27 path consistent with transaction buffering, since a desynced knockback stream is a strong velocity flag on Vulcan and Watchdog.

implementation Implementation Cases

The explosion broadcast that carries both destroyed-block positions and a per-player knockback vector. Every velocity module in the corpus filters it alongside S12PacketEntityVelocity; Rise 6.x dedicates five per-server modes to it, movement modules cancel it to shape knockback, and Tenacity's AntiExploit rate-limits it against crash floods. The packet is one-way, so all client abuse is invisible to the server.

Velocity / AntiKnockback (Memeware 7.3, Nekoware v1 private, Rise 5.99, Sigma 4.11, Spicy, Tenacity 6.0)

Cancel or scale explosion knockback so hits from TNT, creepers and beds apply reduced or zero motion.

PacketReceiveEvent filter: if the packet is S27PacketExplosion, either cancel it outright or multiply motionX/Y/Z by horizontal/vertical percentage settings before the vanilla handler applies them. Tenacity scales in place; Memeware/Nekoware/Spicy cancel; Rise 5.99 and Sigma switch behavior per configured mode.
