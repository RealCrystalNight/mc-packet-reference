Deep Dive

Role in the protocol

S0B broadcasts an entity animation: a VarInt entity id and a byte type (0 swing, 1 damage, 2 leave bed, 3 eat or drink, 4 critical, 5 magic critical, 6 crouch, 7 uncrouch). EntityLivingBase.swingItem sends type 0 through the entity tracker, EntityPlayerMP sends 2 and 3 on bed leave and eating, and attackEntityAsMob sends 4 and 5 for crits. The client handler switches on the type and drives the entity's animation state.

Vanilla handling

NetHandlerPlayClient.handleAnimation looks up the entity and applies the animation: swingProgress for type 0, hurtTime for 1, and so on. Server-side the tracker fans the packet out to every player tracking the entity, so a swing is the one reliable signal that another player is attacking.

Why clients touch it

S0B is the swing ground truth, and the corpus uses it five ways. ClickerMimic (Rise 6.x) records per-player swing intervals and replays them as the client's own clicks. Ranking (Sigma) timestamps swings and attributes deaths to players who swung within 70 ms of the local player taking damage. Rise 5.99 runs its own anticheat off S0B: AutoClickerA counts swings against movement packets, KeepSprintA couples swings to sprint state. PlayerHacks (Rise 5.99) gates its remote aura and nuker on the master's swings, and SocialCreditScore subtracts points for every weapon swing it observes. Nekoware and Rise 5.99 Scaffold import S0B for placement-swing sync without handling it.

Exploit surface

S0B is clientbound only and cannot be spoofed outward, so abuse is observational. A player who hits without producing swings, or with perfectly regular intervals, is an autoclicker; that is exactly what Rise's AutoClickerA and KeepSprintA checks measure on the client's own users. ClickerMimic inverts the trick: it measures a victim's swing rhythm and reproduces it, so the client's clicks inherit a human-looking distribution without any randomization logic of its own.

Server-Side Handling

EntityLivingBase.swingItem sends S0B type 0 to all tracking entities; EntityPlayerMP sends type 2 on bed leave, type 3 on eating, and attackEntityAsMob emits types 4 and 5 for critical hits through func_151248_b. The server applies no rate limit: swings are broadcast as produced, which is what makes swing-count heuristics possible.

Protocol Analysis

Fields: VarInt entity id, unsigned byte animation type. Clientbound in PLAY state. The type byte is read as readUnsignedByte, so values above 7 are legal on the wire and ignored by the vanilla switch. The swing type 0 is the only animation players emit at click rate, which is why every module keys on it specifically.

Anti-Cheat Landscape

Swing-vs-movement ratio is the autoclicker tell: more than 3 swings per movement packet flags AutoClickerA. Keepsprint couples swings to sprint state. The client-side checks in Rise 5.99 are notable because they run in the cheat itself, proof that S0B is the swing source of truth for both sides of the ecosystem.

implementation Implementation Cases

The animation broadcast: entity id plus a type byte, fanned out by the entity tracker. The only reliable signal that another player swung, which makes it the heartbeat of click mimicry, swing-based anticheat checks, combat logging and aura gating in the 8 clients.

ClickerMimic (Rise 6.2.4, Rise 6.1.30)

Record the swing intervals of every visible player and replay them as the client's own clicks, so the local click pattern mirrors the victim's rhythm.

On PacketReceiveEvent, each S0B stamps ticksExisted into a per-name interval list. While the attack key is held, PreUpdateEvent replays the recorded intervals as mc.clickMouse() calls, halved in duration. Sneaking clears the recordings.
