Overview

LiquidSense Dev's Velocity is the classic LiquidBounce knockback controller with eight modes selected by a ListValue: Simple scales or cancels the S12 packet outright, AAC and AACZero defer cancellation into the tick handler with motion rewrites, AACPush divides the incoming XZ motion and drains Y while hurt, Reverse and SmoothReverse preserve or gently damp the knockback through strafe and speedInAir manipulation, AAC4.4.0 halves horizontal motion in air, Jump converts the knockback into a launch, and Glitch cancels the packet and rides noClip for a few ticks. The packet handler also cancels every S27 explosion packet and every S06 health update unconditionally, with a TODO comment noting explosion velocity support is unimplemented.

Packet-side handling

The onPacket handler watches S12PacketEntityVelocity for the self entity. Simple mode rewrites the packet's motion fields: with Horizontal and Vertical both at 0 it cancels the packet entirely, otherwise it scales each motion component by the matching value with integer fixed-point math, so the server still applies a reduced knockback. Glitch cancels the packet only while onGround and sets the velocityInput flag that the update handler turns into noClip. Every other mode just records velocityInput, hurt or the timer reset and defers the actual motion work to the tick handler. The handler also cancels S27PacketExplosion and S06PacketUpdateHealth with no mode check at all, which suppresses explosion knockback and health sync on every mode.

Tick-side modes

The onUpdate handler implements the deferred modes. AAC waits 80ms then multiplies motionX and motionZ by the Horizontal value, leaving Y alone. AACPush divides motionX and motionZ by the AACPushXZReducer while hurtResistantTime is at least 19, subtracts 0.014999993 from motionY each tick while hurt and the Speed module is off, and forces the local onGround true while hurt to fool ground checks. AACZero adds velocity 0, -1, 0 and sets onGround true while hurt, and cancels the JumpEvent during the hurt window. Reverse strafes at the current speed times ReverseStrength while airborne, SmoothReverse sets speedInAir to the SmoothReverseStrength value during the hurt window, and AAC4.4.0 multiplies horizontal motion by 0.6 in air. Jump sets motionY to 0.42 and adds a 0.2 directional boost whenever hurtTime is positive and the player is onGround.

State and disable behavior

velocityInput is the master flag the packet handler arms and the update handler consumes: Reverse and AAC modes check it before acting, SmoothReverse resets speedInAir to 0.02 when it is false, and Glitch assigns noClip = velocityInput each tick so the player becomes non-colliding for exactly as long as the flag stays set. Two MSTimer fields pace the 80ms deferrals, and onDisable resets speedInAir to 0.02F. The JumpEvent handler cancels the vanilla jump for AACPush, AAC4.4.0 and AACZero during the hurt window, which stops the vanilla jump from masking the module's motion work.

What this file does

The Reverse and AACPush modes consume the utility's motion math. getSpeed returns the horizontal magnitude of the current motion, and strafe(speed) rewrites motionX and motionZ as -sin(direction) times speed and cos(direction) times speed, using the client's directional yaw blending from getDirection. Reverse mode calls strafe(MovementUtils.getSpeed() times ReverseStrength) every airborne tick, so the knockback magnitude is preserved along the player's chosen direction, and the same helper underlies the AACPush strafe behavior. The file also carries the potion-aware base-speed helpers used by the client's speed modules, but Velocity only touches getSpeed, strafe and getDirection.

How it counteracts this module

Arc v2 has no dedicated anti-knockback check in this snapshot, and its MovingListener is the honest evidence: onVelocity (line 165) stamps the knockback into VelocityData.addVelocityTime, and the movement checks consume that feed. Flight reads data.getVelocityData().hasVelocity() and the current and last velocity values, and only grants its vertical-move exemption while a knockback is recorded and the player is not on a step, climbing or in liquid; on landing it clears the velocity state. Speed uses the same feed to widen its horizontal expectation around a fresh knockback.

When the LiquidSense module cancels or rewrites the S12, Arc's listener never records the knockback, so Flight's validVerticalMove gate stays false and the post-hit motion is judged against the normal envelope with no knockback allowance. That is the honest mechanism of this entry: the pressure is not a velocity check flagging the cancel, it is the movement checks losing the exemption that a real knockback would have armed, which turns every cancelled hit into ordinary motion to be measured.
