Rise 5.99 Velocity — Full Source Analysis

Analysis

Overview

Rise 5.99's Velocity module intercepts S12PacketEntityVelocity and S27PacketExplosion in onPacketReceive and reshapes or removes the knockback vector before the vanilla handler applies it. Nine modes are selectable: Custom scales the vector by separate horizontal and vertical percentages and cancels the packet when both are zero; Reverse negates the horizontal axes; Vulcan cancels the packet outright and additionally cancels C0F confirmations while hurtTime is positive; Tick Cancel lets the knockback through and then zeroes the client's motion with setVelocity once the Ticks window elapses; Minemenclub cancels the first two knockbacks and zeroes the motion after the third; Stack drops every knockback while stationary and two of every three while moving; Follow First Tick cancels the S12 and re-adds its motion to the next position packet; Ground forces the client-side onGround flag on the tick after a hit; and Redesky combines scaling with a C0B sneak injection and a timer-speed window. An Explosion toggle extends the Custom scaling to S27 explosion knockback, and a More Options group adds delayed horizontal/vertical damping, attack-time damping and a temporary timer boost.

Architecture

All interception funnels through onPacketReceive, which checks the packet type, verifies the entity id against the player, and dispatches on the mode string. The receive handler only touches S12 and S27; everything else is timed in onPreMotion (tick counting, timer-speed windows, delayed damping at hurtTime 8, the Ground onGround fake, the Follow First Tick position offset and the Redesky sneak flush), in onStrafe (the setVelocity(0, 0, 0) zeroing for Tick Cancel and Minemenclub), in onPacketSend (Vulcan's C0F cancellation) and in onAttackEvent (attack-time damping).

State is a handful of fields: ticks, cancelTicks, row, the stored motionX/motionY/motionZ, and the receivingVelocity, countingTicks and lastTick booleans, all reset in onEnable. onUpdateAlwaysInGui hides every mode-specific setting except the active mode's, so the GUI only exposes the sliders that apply. The onlyInAir gate skips the receive handler whenever the player is on the ground and the toggle is on, and the same condition guards the pre-motion logic.

Key techniques

Custom and Redesky scale the packet in place: motionX and motionZ are multiplied by horizontal over 100, motionY by vertical over 100, and the scaled packet replaces the original through event.setPacket, so the vanilla handler applies a reduced vector. When both sliders are zero the packet is cancelled instead, which is the full anti-knockback case. Reverse multiplies only the horizontal axes by -1, turning the push backwards. Follow First Tick cancels the S12, stores the vector divided by 8000, sets motionY to 0.03 and on the next PreMotionEvent adds the stored values to the event's x, y and z, so the knockback is converted into a position delta instead of a velocity. Ground sets thePlayer.onGround to true on the tick after a hit, making movement checks treat the player as grounded when the knockback should have lifted them.

Tick Cancel and Minemenclub defer the zeroing: they count cancelTicks in onPreMotion and, once the threshold (the Ticks setting, default 3) is reached, call setVelocity(0, 0, 0) in onStrafe, but only when Speed, LongJump and Fly are all disabled so the module never fights its own movement modules. Stack uses a row counter: while moving it cancels the first two knockbacks and lets the third through, and while stationary it cancels everything; Minemenclub forces row to 3 when Fly or Speed is active so the zeroing never runs against them.

Mode-specific streams

Redesky is the AAC5-targeted mode: on the S12 it sends a C0BPacketEntityAction START_SNEAKING through addToSendQueueWithoutEvent, cancels the packet and arms receivingVelocity; the next onPreMotion sends STOP_SNEAKING. The sneak pair rides alongside the cancelled knockback so the server sees a sneaking state change around the hit. The same mode, and Custom with More Options, run a timer window: hurtTime == 9 resets ticks, a timerSpeed between 0.1 and 2.0 is applied while ticks stays below timerTicks (0-9), and the speed is restored to 1.0 once the window closes. hurtTime == 8 applies the delayed horizontal/vertical damping to the stored motion, and onAttackEvent applies horizontalAttack and verticalAttack to the live motion while hurtTime is positive.

Vulcan adds its own C0F cancellation while hurtTime is positive, desyncing the transaction stream around the hit, and the Explosion toggle scales or cancels S27 the same way as S12: Custom applies the horizontal/vertical percentages to the explosion offsets, every other mode cancels the packet outright when the toggle is on. The S27 branch only fires when the explosion toggle is enabled, so explosion knockback is untouched by default.

The modes that convert knockback into something else are the notable ones. Ground arms receivingVelocity and fakes onGround on the next tick. Because timerSpeed multiplies the client's tick rate, a value above 1.0 briefly accelerates the game loop after a hit, which the delayed-damping sliders are meant to compensate for.

How it counteracts this module

Grim's KnockbackHandler is a PacketSendListener on ENTITY_VELOCITY that sandwiches the packet between two server transactions: sendTransaction() before the velocity and a second transaction through tasksAfterSend after it. The velocity is recorded into firstBreadMap keyed by the first transaction id, and tickKnockback promotes an entry into lastKnockbackKnownTaken once a transaction with a larger id arrives, which proves the packet reached the client. From that moment the knockback vector is required movement: the prediction engine must see it in the player's motion.

onPredictionComplete compares the prediction offset against offsetToFlag (config Knockback.threshold, default 0.001). A player whose movement lacks the required vector accumulates the offset into a threshold (decay 0.999, ceiling 4) and is set back when the offset is at least 0.1 (immediate-setback-threshold) or the threshold reaches maxAdvantage 1. Among Rise 5.99's modes, Custom at 0/0, Vulcan, Stack while stationary and Minemenclub's zeroed window are direct hits: the required vector never appears and the offset is the full knockback. Tick Cancel's late zeroing also loses because the vector was required from the moment the sandwich confirmed, before the Ticks window elapses. Follow First Tick re-adds the motion as a position delta on the next C03, but Grim predicts velocity, so the instant position jump of the knockback magnitude still diverges from the predicted profile.

The mode this check does not catch is one that also swallows the sandwich. Redesky's sneak pair does not help, since Grim's transaction clock is independent of sneak state. The honest bypass surface is transaction eating: if the client cancels the second transaction receipt and its echo, the velocity is never promoted out of the uncertain firstBreadMap bucket and is never required, so no offset accrues. November's GRIM mode is built exactly this way; the cost is a transaction ledger that diverges from the server's, which Grim's transaction-order accounting and the vanilla inventory/UI confirmations notice over time.

The honest statement is the debug-only status itself: a velocity module today faces NCP's SurvivalFly envelope, not this check, and unused velocity only appears in debug output until the TODO is implemented. Partial reductions leave a partial unused remainder, so the ledger would catch them too; the check as shipped cannot flag anything, which is why this entry documents the direction NCP is heading rather than an active counter.

How it counteracts this module

Spartan's VelocityListener is a MONITOR-priority ProtocolLib adapter on ENTITY_VELOCITY. For the self entity it reads x, y and z through BackPlib.getSafeInt divided by 8000.0, builds a CPlayerVelocityEvent carrying the claimed vector, forwards the cancellation state of the original packet, and runs VelocityEvent.event on the CheckThread. VelocityEvent stores the event as claimedVelocity, pushes it onto the claimedVeloGravity and claimedVeloSpeed queues (each capped at two entries), and calls executeRunners, which dispatches the claimed velocity to Spartan's checks.

The claimed-velocity queues are the compensation layer Spartan's movement checks read: they compare the player's reported movement against the server's velocity vector, with gravity applied for the gravity queue, so a client that cancels the S12 receive-side is still measured against the vector the server sent. The listener itself does not flag; the flagging lives in the movement checks that consume claimedVelocity, which is why this file is the feed rather than the verdict. Because Spartan records the original packet contents, client-side scaling of the S12 fields (Custom and Redesky percentages) does not change what Spartan expects, so partial reduction still shows as a movement deficit.

Among Rise 5.99's modes, every mode that removes or reduces the movement the server's vector predicts (Custom, Vulcan, Tick Cancel, Minemenclub, Stack, Follow First Tick) is measured against the claimed vector. The bypass surface is the queues' size and the ground profile: the last two claims only, and modes that keep the vertical component while removing horizontal movement (Reverse) present movement closer to the claimed gravity profile and stay under the checks' tolerances.
