Overview

AutoWeapon swaps to the strongest sword or tool in the hotbar before every melee attack. An AttackEvent arms a gotIt flag; the outgoing C02PacketUseEntity ATTACK handler then scores every hotbar slot by its generic.attackDamage attribute plus 1.25 per sharpness level (read through ItemUtils.getEnchantment), and when a better weapon exists it cancels the C02 and either switches the client slot directly or hides the swap behind the packet stream. The silent mode, SpoofItem (default off), sends C09PacketHeldItemChange to the best slot, re-emits the cancelled C02 one tick later straight through the NetworkManager, then sends a second C09 restoring the original slot - the server processes the attack while it believes the better weapon is held, and the raw send bypasses the event bus so the replay cannot be re-cancelled.

Attack interception and weapon scoring

onAttack sets gotIt = true; onPacket consumes it: for a C02PacketUseEntity with Action.ATTACK and gotIt set, the module scans hotbar slots 0-8, keeps ItemSword and ItemTool entries, and computes damage = attributeModifiers["generic.attackDamage"] amount + 1.25 * ItemUtils.getEnchantment(stack, Enchantment.sharpness). ItemUtils.getEnchantment walks the stack's enchantment tag list and returns the level of the first matching effect id, so the 1.25 multiplier is sharpness level only - a diamond sword with sharpness III scores 7 + 3.75. When the best slot differs from the current one, the module either queues C09PacketHeldItemChange(slot) and sets spoofedSlot (silent), or writes inventory.currentItem and calls updateController() (visible), then cancels the C02 event and stashes it with tick = 0.

The one-tick replay

A MotionEvent handler with ignoreCondition = true drains the stash: the first pass increments tick to 1 and returns, the second sends the stashed C02 through mc.netHandler.networkManager.sendPacket(packet) - the raw NetworkManager path, which bypasses the client event bus and therefore cannot loop back into onPacket - and, when spoofedSlot is set, queues C09PacketHeldItemChange(currentItem) to restore the original slot. The whole swap-attack-restore sandwich spans two ticks, about 100ms. Because the C09 and the replayed C02 leave in order, the server's held-slot state shows the better weapon when the attack is processed, which is the entire point of the SpoofItem mode: the attack is credited with the sword's damage and the player never visibly switches weapons.

Client-side versus wire behavior

The visible mode is wire-identical to a normal player: the vanilla updateController() syncs the held slot (a single C09 through the normal path) and the attack follows, so nothing about it is detectable beyond normal slot-change traffic. The silent mode is the wire signature: C09 to the best slot, C02 attack, C09 back, all within two ticks, with no swing-to-attack delay in between. The module never adds attacks - one C02 in, one C02 out - so its footprint is purely the item-switch sandwich around each attack.

What this file does

The module is four state fields and three event handlers. gotIt, packetUseEntity, spoofedSlot and tick drive a small state machine: the AttackEvent arms it, the C02 packet handler decides and stashes, and the MotionEvent drains the stash exactly one tick later. The scoring loop in onPacket is the decision core - it iterates the hotbar, filters swords and tools, reads the generic.attackDamage attribute modifier and adds 1.25 per sharpness level through ItemUtils.getEnchantment. How it counteracts this module

Arc v2's KillAura runs from the FightListener on every attack and combines four sub-checks. The direction check computes the angle between the player's look vector and the attacked entity and flags anything above max-direction-angle; the multi sub-check compares the angles to the last and current attacked entity and flags when they differ by more than maxAngle while both are in reach; the reach sub-check measures the player-to-entity distance minus the entity's velocity, so knockback does not mask an out-of-range hit; and the speed sub-check counts attacks and attack packets per second against max-attacks and max-attack-packets, resetting on a one-second boundary.\n\nFor AutoWeapon the check bounds the attack stream rather than catching the swap itself: the replayed C02 must still satisfy the direction angle and the reach bound, and the no-swing sub-check can fire because the module cancels and replays the attack - the swing animation the client shows belongs to the original event, while the C02 arrives one tick later. The speed sub-check is neutral here because the module emits exactly one C02 per attack; the value of the check is the combined stream when AutoWeapon runs alongside Aura, where the same listener scores the aura's attacks and the replay timing together.
