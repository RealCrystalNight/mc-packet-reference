Overview

Rise 6.1.30's KillAura is the combat core of the 6.x branch: three attack modes (Single, Switch, Multiple), four rotation modes (Legit/Normal, Snap, NCP, Autistic AntiCheat), fourteen auto-block modes, and a full movement-correction layer. All aiming funnels through the shared RotationComponent engine, which multiplies the configured rotation speed by 36, walks a randomized randomAngle offset toward the target, gates every intermediate rotation with a ray-cast predicate so the smoothed path stays inside the hitbox, and fixes the GCD with RotationUtil.applySensitivityPatch on every smoothing iteration. Attacks dispatch through playerController.attackEntity or a raw C02PacketUseEntity when keepSprint is on; the block/unblock pair emits C08/C07, and the Grim, Watchdog 1.17, New NCP and Old Intave modes swap held-item slots with paired C09 packets to desync the server's view of the sword.

Architecture

The module is event-driven with priority-ordered listeners. onHighPreUpdate (Priorities.HIGH) is the target and rotation pass: it refreshes the mouse over, computes allowAttack from BadPacketsComponent (unless the bad-packets check is disabled), decays the attack counter, applies the expandRange heuristic (every 20 ticks it sets expandRange to 3 + Math.random() * 0.5, a 3.0-3.5 block range expansion used when no targets are found), calls getTargets() which delegates to TargetComponent.getTargets(range), picks targets.get(0), runs preBlock() for the auto-block mode, then rotations(). onMediumPriorityPreUpdate carries no priority tag and therefore runs after other modules have had their chance to override the rotations; only then does it call doAttack(targets) and postAttackBlock(), so the module attacks with the final rotation state of the tick. onPreMotionEvent flushes the packetQueue (a ConcurrentLinkedQueue of packets queued by the blink and ping-spoof block modes) via sendNoEvent, increments hitTicks, clears blocking when the held item is not a sword, and runs the Watchdog auto-block cycle which spoofs mc.objectMouseOver from RotationComponent.lastRotations before calling mc.rightClickMouse() so the server sees a right click aimed at the last rotation. onPacketSend tracks swing state (C0A sets swing true, C03 resets it) and dispatches the Intave packetBlock mode, which cancels the outgoing C03, unblocks, re-sends the C03 without events, and re-blocks.

Rotation engine

All aiming goes through RotationComponent.setRotations(rotations, rotationSpeed, correctMovement, raycast), which stores the target, multiplies rotationSpeed by 36, and calls smooth(). smooth() only runs its randomization when a raycast predicate was supplied and the target differs from the current rotations by more than 5 degrees: it accumulates a randomAngle offset whose sign flips every 10 ticks (ticksExisted / 10 % 2 == 0 ? -1 : 1), advances the offset by sin/cos of that angle scaled by speed = (Math.random()^3) * 20, and after each advance applies the raycast predicate (in KillAura, RayCastUtil.rayCast(rotations, range, -0.1f) must hit an entity). A miss inverts the walk direction and retries; a second miss zeroes the offset and falls back to targetRotations plus up to 2 degrees of jitter. The smoothed result comes from RotationUtil.smooth, which distributes the per-tick speed across yaw and pitch proportionally to the remaining deltas, adds per-iteration micro-jitter (yaw += (random - 0.5) / 1000, pitch -= random / 200) for FPS/20 plus random iterations, and applies applySensitivityPatch to every iteration so the final stream stays on the mouse-sensitivity GCD grid. The component's onPreMotionEvent listener writes the smoothed rotations into the outgoing motion event (event.setYaw/Pitch), sets rotationYawHead and renderPitchHead, and records lastServerRotations; when the smoothed rotations come within 1 degree of the player's real rotations it deactivates and calls correctDisabledRotations(), which snaps the view back onto the GCD grid via RotationUtil.resetRotation(RotationUtil.applySensitivityPatch(rotations, lastRotations)). MovementFix is an enum with OFF, NORMAL (Rise), TRADITIONAL and BACKWARDS_SPRINT: NORMAL rewrites the input yaw on MoveInputEvent through MoveUtil.fixMovement and sets movementYaw/velocityYaw to the smoothed yaw on StrafeEvent and JumpEvent, TRADITIONAL only sets yaw on StrafeEvent and JumpEvent, and BACKWARDS_SPRINT force-releases the sprint key whenever the smoothed yaw differs from MoveUtil.direction() by more than 45 degrees. The NCP rotation mode additionally predicts both entities' motion, applies applySensitivityPatch to the computed axis, and with 10% probability feeds RotationComponent a random offset of up to 10 yaw and 3 pitch instead.

Attack dispatch and autoblock

doAttack() gates on the attackStopWatch/clickStopWatch pair: clicks are drawn from cps.getRandomBetween() * 1.5 (so the 1-20 bounds produce 1.5-30 clicks per second), nextSwing becomes 1000 / clicks, and the attack fires when attackStopWatch.finished(nextSwing) and (clickStopWatch.finished(delay * 50) or the Hit Select flag) hold, with a nonsense extra gate Math.sin(nextSwing) + 1 > Math.random(). attack() posts an AttackEvent on the bus, calls the (empty) attackBlock(), swings via swingItem(), then either playerController.attackEntity or, with keepSprint on, syncCurrentPlayItem plus a direct C02PacketUseEntity ATTACK send with a critical-hit check when falling. Single and Switch modes attack the primary target when it is within range (or when rayCast is on and the ray hits it), fall through to attack whatever entity the ray cast actually hit when it is not a fireball, and otherwise abort if curBlockDamageMP is nonzero; Multiple mode removes out-of-range targets and attacks every survivor in one tick. Auto-block modes range from pure rendering (Fake/None cancel the RightClickEvent and force EnumAction.BLOCK through onRenderItem) through post-attack C08 blocks (Vanilla, Imperfect Vanilla, Vanilla ReBlock), C09 slot-swap pairs (Grim, Watchdog 1.17, New NCP, Old Intave), the Universal blockTicks 2-3 cycle with PingSpoofComponent.blink()/dispatch(), and the Intave C03 cancel-resend-block sequence.

What this file does

This is the shared rotation engine every combat module drives. setRotations() stores the target rotations, multiplies the caller's rotationSpeed by 36, stores the MovementFix mode and the optional raycast predicate, sets active, and immediately calls smooth(). smooth() is where the anti-detection work happens: when a raycast predicate is present and the target is more than 5 degrees away from the current rotations, it builds a randomized walk. The walk speed is (Math.random() * Math.random() * Math.random()) * 20, and randomAngle accumulates (20 + (random - 0.5) * random^3 * 360) with the whole term multiplied by -1 or +1 depending on whether ticksExisted / 10 is even, so the walk direction flips every 10 ticks. Each step advances a persistent offset vector by -sin(randomAngle) on x and cos(randomAngle) on y scaled by speed, adds it to the target, and tests the result with the raycast predicate; a miss sets randomAngle to the atan2-based reverse direction and re-advances the offset, and a second miss zeroes the offset and falls back to the raw target plus up to 2 degrees of random jitter. The final rotations come from RotationUtil.smooth(targetYaw, targetPitch, rotationSpeed + Math.random()), which interpolates with GCD-fixed per-iteration steps.

The component's event listeners apply the result everywhere the game reads the look: onLook overwrites the render event's rotations, onPreMotionEvent writes yaw/pitch into the outgoing motion packet, sets mc.thePlayer.rotationYawHead and renderPitchHead to the smoothed values, and records lastServerRotations so other code can see exactly what was sent. The movement fix is applied on MoveInputEvent (MoveUtil.fixMovement with the smoothed yaw for MovementFix.NORMAL), on StrafeEvent and JumpEvent (yaw override for NORMAL and TRADITIONAL, plus BACKWARDS_SPRINT on jump), and BACKWARDS_SPRINT additionally force-releases the sprint key when the smoothed yaw differs from the movement direction by more than 45 degrees, so the client does not sprint into the target. When the smoothed rotations come within 1 degree of the player's real rotations the component deactivates and correctDisabledRotations() snaps the view back onto the GCD grid using RotationUtil.resetRotation(RotationUtil.applySensitivityPatch(rotations, lastRotations)), which is what prevents the visible snap-back when the aura releases the look.

The rotation speed of every module is effectively capped by the * 36 multiplier in setRotations: KillAura's Rotation speed setting runs 0-10, so the effective per-tick smoothing speed is 0-360 with the + Math.random() jitter added on top. smooth() also writes rotations.x into movementYaw for NORMAL/TRADITIONAL and velocityYaw unconditionally, and finishes with mc.entityRenderer.getMouseOver(1) so the client-side mouse-over reflects the smoothed look immediately. The smoothed flag prevents re-smoothing within the same tick, and onPreUpdate re-initializes any null rotation state from the player's actual yaw/pitch so the engine self-heals when a module enables it mid-tick.

What this file does

The math behind every rotation. calculate(from, to) is the plain atan2 look: yaw = atan2(diffZ, diffX) - 90, pitch = -atan2(diffY, horizontalDistance). The entity overload aims at the target's custom position vector plus a vertical offset clamped between 0 and 90% of the entity's bounding box height, so the aim point tracks the victim's eye band instead of its feet. The adaptive overload used by KillAura's Legit/Normal and Snap modes first computes the normal rotations and ray-casts them at the given range; if that hits an entity it returns immediately, otherwise it sweeps a grid of candidate aim points, decrementing yPercent from 1 to 0 in steps of 0.25 + random * 0.1 and x/z percents from 1 to -0.5 in steps of 0.5, and returns the first grid point whose ray cast hits an entity, falling back to the normal rotations if none do. That sweep is what lets the module aim at an exposed edge or chest of a target whose head ray is blocked.

applySensitivityPatch is the GCD fix. It computes mouseSensitivity = (mc.gameSettings.mouseSensitivity * (1 + Math.random() / 10000000) * 0.6 + 0.2), then multiplier = sensitivity^3 * 8.0 * 0.15 (equivalently sensitivity^3 * 1.2), and rounds the rotation delta to the nearest multiple of that multiplier relative to the previous rotation, clamping pitch to -90..90. The tiny random perturbation on sensitivity (1 + random/1e7) varies the multiplier imperceptibly so the quantized deltas do not form an exact repeating pattern. Every smoothing iteration in smooth() applies the patch, which is what keeps the whole yaw/pitch stream on the grid a real mouse at that sensitivity could produce. The NCP rotation mode applies it to the predicted axis before handing it to the engine.

smooth()/move() implement the actual interpolation: move() distributes the per-tick speed across yaw and pitch in proportion to the remaining deltas (so a mostly-yaw target turn spends most of the speed on yaw), and smooth() applies move() then loops for (Minecraft.getDebugFPS() / 20f + Math.random() * 10) iterations, adding micro-jitter (yaw += (random - 0.5) / 1000, pitch -= random / 200) and re-patching the GCD each iteration, finally clamping pitch. resetRotation() and relateToPlayerRotation() wrap the delta into the -180..180 range relative to the player's real rotation, and resetRotation() is what RotationComponent.correctDisabledRotations() uses to return the view to the player's actual yaw/pitch on the GCD grid when the aura stops aiming.

What this file does

The enum that selects how the rotation engine rewrites the player's movement input while the aura is aiming. OFF leaves the input untouched, which is the default KillAura setting (movementCorrection.setDefault(MovementFix.OFF)). NORMAL (displayed as Rise) rewrites the movement input yaw to the smoothed rotations.yaw via MoveUtil.fixMovement on MoveInputEvent and additionally forces movementYaw and velocityYaw to the smoothed yaw, which keeps strafe and forward input aligned with the aim direction so the player moves exactly where the aura looks without the server seeing movement that disagrees with the yaw in the motion packets. TRADITIONAL applies the yaw override only on StrafeEvent and JumpEvent without the MoveInput rewrite, the classic fix used by older clients. BACKWARDS_SPRINT releases the sprint key whenever the smoothed yaw is more than 45 degrees away from the movement direction, preventing the player from sprinting toward a target the aura is not actually facing while allowing sprint in the aim direction.

The enum is constructed with Lombok @AllArgsConstructor and stores a display name per constant, so the KillAura's movementCorrection ListValue can iterate MovementFix.values() in the constructor to populate the dropdown. The rotation engine branches on the enum in three places: onMove (NORMAL only), onStrafe and onJump (NORMAL and TRADITIONAL, plus BACKWARDS_SPRINT on jump), and smooth() (NORMAL and TRADITIONAL set movementYaw). BACKWARDS_SPRINT is handled in onPreUpdate by comparing Math.abs(rotations.x % 360 - Math.toDegrees(MoveUtil.direction()) % 360) against 45 degrees and pressing/releasing the sprint bind accordingly.

The distinction between NORMAL and TRADITIONAL matters for anticheat geometry: NORMAL rewrites the raw input so the client-side movement vector and the sent yaw always agree, which keeps serverside prediction consistent, while TRADITIONAL only touches the strafe/jump yaw and leaves the input vector alone, which is the mode for servers that compare the pre-move yaw with the post-move delta. OFF is the mode that lets the player move in any direction relative to the aim, which the movement checks on strict servers flag when the aura spins the yaw faster than the movement can follow.

NCP's Angle check scores the attack stream for forcefield behavior. KillAura's Multiple mode is the clean hit: it attacks every in-range target from the same position in the same tick, so averageMove is near zero (standing killaura), averageTime is near zero, and each victim id change with a yaw rotation across the target ring increments averageSwitching. The Legit/Normal smoothing walk at default speed stays under the yaw limit, which is why the check is most effective against the Switch and Multiple modes and the two aggressive rotation modes. The Switch mode compounds the problem: it cycles through pastTargets exclusions (when the eviction list is actually populated) and re-targets, which produces idDiffLast transitions with large yaw deltas, feeding the switchspeed counter. A module that stays on one target, moves between hits and uses the Legit/Normal walk can keep all four averages inside their thresholds, so the check rewards movement-between-attacks and low yaw deltas rather than raw distance.

How it counteracts this module

NCP's fight.Reach measures the attack from the server-tracked player position to the victim and compares against reachSurvivalDistance (default 3.0). The victim y is refined to the attacker's eye-height band (clamped between the victim's foot level and its height), the precision inset (centertoedge, half the victim width projected along the attack direction when reachPrecision is on) is subtracted, and a per-entity size modifier applies (6.5 for dragons, 1.5 for giants). A positive violation accumulates into reachVL and executes the configured actions; the dynamic reachMod adaption then tightens the limit for repeat offenders: every hit within the reduce band steps reachMod down toward distanceMin, so a KillAura that consistently attacks at 3.1-3.4 gets silently cancelled even though the nominal limit is 3.0 plus inset, and the attackPenalty applies an attack cooldown on cancels.

KillAura's range value of 3-6 maps directly onto this: the default 3.0 setting attacks at the limit, and any higher setting exceeds it outright. The module's own distance gate is client-side getDistanceToEntity <= range with no subtraction for the victim's girth, while NCP's inset grants only the half-width toward the attacker, so the server-side measurement is strictly more demanding than the client's own gate. Unlike the 5.99 branch there is no Extended Range mode that also rewrites the movement stream, so the server-tracked position cannot be shortened to dodge the measurement; the 6.1.30 module only manipulates rotations and attack packets.

The reachMod adaption is what makes the default setting risky too: even at 3.0 range, the small positive violations from the missing inset and from aiming at the eye band while the server clamps the victim y step reachMod down, and once reachMod shrinks the silent-cancel branch (lenpRel - distanceLimit * reachMod > 0) starts cancelling attacks that were previously under the limit. The Universal auto-block cycle adds nothing to the distance but its PingSpoofComponent transaction spoofing inflates the measured lag, and TickTask.getLag(1000, true) only applies when lag is below 1.5, so sustained spoofed pings keep the violation accumulating without the lag dampener.

How it counteracts this module

Arc's KillAura check runs from the FightListener on every C02 attack and combines five detectors. Direction computes the angle between the player's look direction and the vector to the attacked entity and flags over max-direction-angle. Multi compares the angle to the current and previous attacked entity and flags when the angle difference exceeds the limit while both entities are within maxReach of each other. NoSwing flags when more than maxSwingTime passes without an arm swing. Reach measures the distance to the entity minus its current velocity (so knockback does not false-flag) against maxReach. Speed counts attacks per second against maxAttacks, and the separate checkFrequency() counts raw attack packets per second against maxAttackPackets.

KillAura's Multiple mode is the direct target: attacking several entities in one tick means the single look vector can only satisfy the Direction check for one of them, and the angle difference between the attacked entities trips the Multi branch immediately. The module's noSwing option (a BooleanValue under the Advanced section, default false) removes the swing packet entirely when enabled, which trips NoSwing; even with it off, the auto-block modes that force the block animation between hits can exceed maxSwingTime if the swing is delayed past the limit. The cps bounds up to 20 multiplied by 1.5 produce 30 clicks per second, over any default maxAttacks and maxAttackPackets, so the Speed branch flags sustained high-cps use.

The Autistic AntiCheat rotation mode, which rotates yaw by rotationSpeed * 10 per tick while pitching 0, is caught by the Direction angle since the look vector is usually far from the target when the attack fires. The Legit/Normal mode with the smoothing walk survives Direction because the ray-cast predicate keeps the look inside the hitbox, but the Reach detector still measures the true distance and the Speed detector still counts the packet rate, so the module cannot rely on its rotation engine to clear the check entirely; silent rotations neutralize Direction and NoSwing, not Reach and Speed.

How it counteracts this module

Hawk's FightHitbox replays each attack from the packet-observed yaw/pitch. Because 1.8 sends the C02 before the yaw update, Hawk stores the InteractEntityEvent in lastInteractionMap and defers the geometry to the next MoveEvent, then builds the direction from the stored yaw/pitch (the HawkPlayer's tracked yaw for 1.8 clients, the move's own yaw otherwise). The victim is rewound to its ping-ago position via the lag compensator, the victim AABB is expanded by BOX_EPSILON 0.005, and a ray from the attacker eye (with the sneaking -0.08 offset and vehicle eye-height handling) is intersected against the box. No intersection punishes 'Did not hit hitbox.' when CHECK_BOX_INTERSECTION is on; an intersection beyond MAX_REACH (3.1 survival, 5.0 creative) punishes as reach; with CHECK_OCCLUSION a BlockIterator flags hits that travel through blocks as 'Interacted through <block>'.

KillAura's throughWalls mode is the direct target: when throughWalls is on (and rayCast off) the module expands the target's bounding box by 0.1 and attacks on any intercept, which is exactly the geometry Hawk re-derives from the server direction without that expansion. A wall hit either misses the victim box entirely (the HITBOX branch) or the intercept lands beyond MAX_REACH (the reach branch), and the occlusion iterator names the wall it went through. The Multiple mode attacking several entities in one tick fails the direction requirement for all but the one the look vector happens to cross, so each extra victim yields a miss. The rayCast-on mode (RayCastUtil.rayCast with a -0.1f box expansion in the rotation predicate) survives only when the server-observed rotations genuinely cross the rewound victim box within 3.1, which is the Legit/Normal rotation mode at default range.

The lag compensation is the check's weak spot against this module: it rewinds the victim to ping-ago, but the module's attack timing is already driven by its own clickStopWatch and the rotation engine, so a victim moving fast can desync from the rewound box and produce a miss that is punished as a hitbox violation rather than a clean hit; conversely, hits that land exactly inside the rewound box at 3.0-3.1 pass, which is why default-range Legit/Normal play is the only configuration the check consistently clears.
